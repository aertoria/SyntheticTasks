# Interaction-structure complexification: stacking tasks beyond the context window (memory RL), sharded and underspecified multi-turn tasks, cooperative multi-agent tasks, and adversarial injections in environments

*Scope: operators that make an already-verifiable easy seed harder by changing **how it is delivered** (horizon, turns, who holds which information, what hostile content sits in the environment), not what it asks. Compiled 2026-09-30. Verification: 28 researcher entries checked against primary sources (arXiv full text via HTML/PDF, Meta publication page for Coral). 16 corrected, 1 dropped (SpyRL/RLSVR, already covered in note 17), 5 added (IterResearch, InfoPO, future-turn clarification preferences, SPAG, Adaptive Attacks Break Defenses). Unverifiable sub-claims were removed.*

Cross-references, not re-covered here: RULER, BABILong, QwenLong-L1.5, LoongRL and other reading-only long-context padding (note 07); SPIRAL, TextArena and zero-sum game self-play (notes 05, 09); missing-parameter clarification in Magnet/RODS/COVERT, tau2-bench dual control, and STAC/ToolHazard harmful chains (note 06); SEAD, simulator-fidelity diagnostics and SpyRL/RLSVR task-to-spy-game transformation (note 17); Rainbow Teaming (note 14).

## TL;DR

- **Changing delivery keeps the label for free, so prove it with a control.** In Lost-in-Conversation, the CONCAT control (every shard in one turn) scores 95.1% of the fully specified FULL setting, while SHARDED loses 39% on average across 15 LLMs. Treat any item whose CONCAT or oracle view fails as a label error, not a hard item. This is the cheapest way to bring back GRPO variance on seeds that are already saturated.
- **Under RL, "train short, test long" works for every horizon knob; under SFT it does not.** Examples: MEM1 trained on 2 objectives beats Qwen2.5-14B at 16. MemAct trains on ≤3 objectives and scores 54.3% at 8. MemAgent trains on 32K/60K-token documents and keeps 71.09% at 3.5M. Mem-α trains on ≤30K tokens and generalizes past 400K. IterResearch trains at T_max=32 and scores 42.5% on BrowseComp-200 at T_max=2048 (vs 15.2% at 32). MEM1's SFT baseline collapses beyond 6 objectives (8-objective EM 0.027 vs 1.870 for RL). Keep the training knob small and cheap, and evaluate far past it.
- **Filter out items answerable without the context before padding or stacking.** MemAgent dropped about 50% of 80K HotpotQA questions because Qwen2.5-7B answered them 100% best-of-2 closed-book. Otherwise the reward pays for parametric recall, not memory.
- **Sharding needs a competence-gated curriculum plus bounded memory.** In RLAAR's ablation on Qwen3-1.7B, a shard curriculum gated at ρ=0.8 of the single-turn reward reaches the final stage in 90 steps and scores 71.9 LiC. The same curriculum needs 1000+ steps at ρ=1.0, and scores 63.2 without any curriculum and 40.9 at ρ=0.6. Rolling-memory RL beats full-history RL by 15.2–35.6 pp. When RL signal is scarce, distill the model's own fully specified view into its sharded view: FiC recovers ≥92% of single-turn performance, and MAIGO raises the SHARDED/FULL ratio from 66.5% to 84.1%.
- **Hiding the spec behind an oracle user is a large, cheap difficulty source.** Removing information categories from SWE-bench Verified issues drops resolution from 43.8% to 23.7%. Learned clarification (CLARITI) recovers 36.8% with 3.0 questions, versus GPT-5 asking 5.1. Credit the turns that actually change the next action: InfoPO's counterfactual information gain beats GRPO-based baselines by 14–16%. Restrict the simulator to the withheld spec, or the task quietly becomes easy again.
- **Any operator that can make a task unsolvable needs an explicit, balanced reward for asking or stopping.** Stock evaluators give zero credit for correct abstention (BENCH2ROBUST's S3 episodes). Without partial credit, 38% of BENCH2ROBUST training tasks had zero reward variance. Degenerate constant policies (always ask, always abstain, always refuse) are the default failure.
- **Adversarial content in untrusted slots of existing capability environments is the most durable difficulty source.** More capable models are not automatically harder to attack: AgentDojo reports inverse scaling, and Gemini found better instruction-followers sometimes easier to attack. Static templates saturate fast. Adaptive attacks broke all 8 evaluated IPI defenses at >50% ASR, and AutoInject's RL suffixes break Meta-SecAlign-70B. Use learned attackers against a population of defenders (GPT-Red, ARLAS, CoER).
- **Co-hardening capability and safety works when the adversarial task is built on a capability task and the defender is rewarded for completion AND resistance.** CoER takes utility from 63.2% to 76.3% while attack success falls from 38.5% to 0.2% (its own seven-domain eval). GPT-Red converts OpenAI capability environments into attacker-editable ones. This is the closest existing answer to note 06's co-hardening gap.
- **Every adversarial operator needs benign twins.** In IH-Challenge, removing the anti-over-refusal split drops the over-refusal score from 0.950 to 0.831 and helpfulness from 0.773 to 0.613. OR-Bench finds a 0.89 rank correlation between refusing toxic and refusing seemingly toxic prompts.
- **Long, multi-party episodes need segment-, turn- or role-aware credit assignment, or the gradient signal fades.** Tools: MemAgent's multi-conversation advantage, ReSum-GRPO broadcasting, UMA's task-stratified normalization, MARSHAL's turn-level sum-then-normalize. In cooperative Hanabi, SPIRAL-style role-conditioned advantages drop Mini Hanabi from 54.33% to 24.93%.

## Method notes

**Family A — horizon beyond the context window (memory and context-management RL)**

### MEM1 (+ MemAct) — MEM1: Learning to Synergize Memory and Reasoning for Efficient Long-Horizon Agents (Zhou et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15841 · companion: Memory as Action: Autonomous Context Curation for Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks (Zhang et al., 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12635
- **Mechanism**: MEM1 interleaves several independent questions from Search-R1's HotpotQA+NQ multi-hop set into one composite query. The agent must search for each and answer all of them. At every turn it writes a consolidated internal state (`<IS>`), and earlier `<IS>/<query>/<info>` are deleted, so context stays nearly flat. It is trained from Qwen2.5-7B Base with PPO, using a 2D attention mask to make token log-probabilities valid under the pruned context. MemAct builds the same multi-objective QA (following MEM1) plus single-objective ASearcher tasks. Its memory action is **Prune&Write**: remove history turns by ID and write a summary note. It is trained with DCPO, which splits trajectories into logically consistent segments.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Composition adds no new facts but multiplies the search and bookkeeping horizon. Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct scores EM-sum 0.732 of 2 at 2 objectives (≈37% per item) and 0.567 of 16 at 16 objectives (≈3.5% per item).
- **Correctness / verification**: Per-item gold answers carry over. MEM1's reward is 1 point per correct answer (semicolon-separated). It is 0 if XML tags are mismatched or the number of answers differs from the number of questions. No format reward: a tag-format penalty sped convergence but lowered final EM (0.466 vs 0.709). MemAct scores with an LLM judge using three-pass consensus. Its reward is +1 for success and −0.1 for exceeding 20K context tokens or 40 steps.
- **Difficulty control**: Number of objectives N (MEM1 trains at 2 and tests at 1–16; MemAct trains at ≤3 and tests up to 8), plus token and step budgets.
- **Reported results**: MEM1-QA at 16 objectives: EM 1.97, peak context 10.4×10² tokens. Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct: 0.567, 38.4×10². Search-R1: 0.009. MEM1 is "3.5× performance, 3.7× less memory" vs 14B. RL vs SFT (SFT on GPT-4o trajectories, also given 1- and 3-objective data): 0.709 vs 0.433 at 2 objectives, 1.630 vs 0.088 at 6, 1.870 vs 0.027 at 8, 1.900 vs 0.000 at 16. MemAct-RL-14B: 59.1% multi-objective accuracy vs Qwen3-235B 53.1% and Tongyi-DeepResearch 56.0%, at about 51% lower average token cost (8.2×10⁴ vs 16.7×10⁴). At 8 objectives it scores 54.3% vs Search-R1 39.3%. RL lifts MemAct from 0.485 (SFT) to 0.591.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: The stacked items are independent, so the task trains bookkeeping, not integration. Summed EM gives partial credit. At 2 objectives MEM1 (0.709) is slightly below Qwen2.5-14B (0.732). The learned memory does not transfer to other memory competencies: Mem-α reports MEM1 at 0.071 on MemoryAgentBench.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Pack k saturated items (QA, math, tool calls) into one episode under a fixed memory or turn budget, and reward per item. Start at k=2–3 and raise k when group pass rates saturate. To test integration, chain the items so answer i is an input to item i+1, and compute the chained gold programmatically.

### MemAgent — MemAgent: Reshaping Long-Context LLM with Multi-Conv RL-based Memory Agent (Yu et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02259
- **Mechanism**: Following RULER, gold HotpotQA paragraphs are embedded among distractor paragraphs from the same dataset. The agent runs in an 8K window split into 1,024 tokens of query, 5,000 of chunk, 1,024 of memory and 1,024 of output. It reads chunks sequentially, overwrites its memory after each, and answers from memory. Multi-Conv DAPO treats each chunk step as an independent conversation and applies the final-answer advantage to all of them.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: An easy question with visible evidence becomes a stream-reading and selective-retention problem whose length can exceed any context window.
- **Correctness / verification**: The reward is `1[is_equiv(y, ŷ)]` against the original gold. Evaluation uses sub_em, where an answer must contain every gold element. Of 80,000 HotpotQA training questions, about 50% were removed because Qwen2.5-7B Base or Instruct answered them 100% best-of-2 without context.
- **Difficulty control**: Document length (number of distractors), chunk and memory size, and a two-stage length curriculum. Stage I uses 32,768 instances of about 32K tokens. Stage II uses 2,560 instances of up to 60K tokens: DocQA-RL-1.6K mixed with Stage I data.
- **Reported results**: RL-MemAgent-14B on RULER-HotpotQA scores 80.47 at 7K, 81.25 at 112K, 79.69 at 448K, 78.91 at 1.75M and 71.09 at 3.5M tokens. Qwen2.5-Instruct-14B falls from 75.00 at 7K to 2.34 at 896K. MemAgent scores >95% on 512K RULER NIAH.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Same-corpus distractors can accidentally contain alternative answers. Overwrite memory is lossy. The skill is single-competency: Mem-α reports 0.198 on MemoryAgentBench.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: For any context-grounded QA seed, pad with same-distribution distractors to multiples of the window. Drop closed-book-answerable items, string-check that distractors do not contain the gold, and grow length in stages.

### Mem-α (+ MemoryAgentBench) — Mem-α: Learning Memory Construction via Reinforcement Learning (Wang et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25911 · MemoryAgentBench: Evaluating Memory in LLM Agents via Incremental Multi-Turn Interactions (Hu et al., 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257
- **Mechanism**: Instances are streams of chunks from 8 datasets grouped by competency. Accurate retrieval: SQuAD, HotpotQA, PerLTQA, LongMemEval. Test-time learning: PubMed-RCT, NLU, TREC-Coarse, where labelled examples stream in and later queries need the learned mapping. Long-range understanding: BookSum chapters split into 10–20 chunks. The agent writes to core, episodic and semantic memory via tools. The reward is r₁ (QA correctness) + r₂ (tool-call success) + β·r₃ (compression 1 − l_mem/l_chunks) + γ·r₄ (Qwen3-32B-validated memory-operation quality). Correctness comes from a decoupled pipeline: BM25 retrieval plus a fixed generator over the final memory. MemoryAgentBench converts long-context datasets into incremental chunk streams across four competencies. Its new FactConsolidation dataset orders MQuAKE counterfactual edit pairs so the newer, contradicting fact has a larger serial number, and asks single- or multi-hop questions whose gold is the newest fact.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Questions arrive only after the stream, so the agent must decide what to store without knowing the query. Test-time-learning conversion turns trivial classification into in-context rule acquisition held in memory.
- **Correctness / verification**: Labels come from the source datasets (EM/F1, source-based metrics; LLM judge for LongMemEval). Delivery changes only timing.
- **Difficulty control**: Chunk count and length, questions per instance, competency mix, and context length (FactConsolidation is built at 6K, 32K, 64K and 262K).
- **Reported results**: 4,139 compiled instances were stratified down to 562 training instances (three days on 32 H100s). On the MemoryAgentBench evaluation suite (9 datasets, 112 instances), Mem-α-4B averages 0.592. Baselines: long-context Qwen3-32B 0.461, BM25 RAG-top2 0.502, MemAgent 0.198, MEM1 0.071. Trained on ≤30K tokens, it generalizes to >400K (13×).
- **Limitations / failure modes**: The memory-quality reward is LLM-judged and hackable. Conflict resolution was excluded from training. The dataset is small.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Turn cheap classification or QA seeds into "learn-then-answer" streams. Generate contradiction or update chains (FactConsolidation-style) to train overwrite and forgetting. Reward memory only through a frozen reader's accuracy, so the memory writer cannot grade itself.

### LongMemEval + LoCoMo (+ Memory-R1) — LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term Interactive Memory (Wu et al., 2024)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813 · LoCoMo: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17753 · Memory-R1: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19828
- **Mechanism**: LongMemEval uses Llama-3-70B to write attribute-focused user backgrounds and propose seed QA pairs. Human experts filter and rewrite all 500 questions and decompose answers into evidence statements. Each statement is embedded, indirectly, in a self-chat "evidence session" that humans screen. Histories mix evidence sessions with shuffled filler in a fixed ratio: 25% ShareGPT, 25% UltraChat, 50% simulated sessions on non-conflicting attributes. Timestamps are ordered where needed. Standard settings are S (about 115K tokens per question) and M (500 sessions, about 1.5M tokens). The five abilities tested are extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge update, and abstention (30 false-premise questions). LoCoMo generates 50 dialogues grounded in personas and temporal event graphs, averaging 300 turns and 9K tokens over up to 35 sessions, with human verification. Memory-R1 turns LoCoMo into RL episodes. A Memory Manager chooses ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP and is rewarded by a frozen Answer Agent's EM; the Answer Agent is trained on EM too.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: The same fact becomes a needle across sessions, with temporal anchoring, superseded values and unanswerable premises.
- **Correctness / verification**: Evidence is human-screened and fillers are chosen to be non-conflicting. The GPT-4o judge agrees with human experts more than 97% of the time. Memory-R1 uses EM against gold.
- **Difficulty control**: Number of filler sessions, evidence position, number of evidence sessions per question, and question type.
- **Reported results**: Long-context LLMs drop 30–60% from oracle evidence to LongMemEval_S. In a simpler manual setting, ChatGPT and Coze memory drop 37% and 64% relative to offline reading. Memory-R1 trains on 152 QA pairs. On LLaMA-3.1-8B, Memory-R1-GRPO improves F1 by 28.5%, BLEU-1 by 34.0% and judge score by 30.2% (relative) over the strongest baseline (MemoryOS in the current version). It generalizes to MSC and LongMemEval.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Small question pools (500; LoCoMo about 50 dialogues) invite contamination if used for training. LoCoMo alone fits in modern windows. Partly judge-scored.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Use the pipeline as a generator. Render verifiable seed facts and programmatic update chains as evidence sessions, scale filler, and hold out the public sets for evaluation. Reward memory writes via a frozen reader's EM.

### AgeMem — Agentic Memory: Learning Unified Long-Term and Short-Term Memory Management for Large Language Model Agents (Yu et al., 2026)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01885
- **Mechanism**: One HotpotQA item is spread over three stages. Stage 1 is a conversation exposing the supporting facts (the agent may store them in long-term memory). Stage 2 resets short-term context, keeping long-term memory, then floods it with distractor messages. Stage 3 asks the question. Store, retrieve, update, summarize and discard are tools. It is trained with a three-stage progressive RL schedule and step-wise GRPO that sends the final reward back to earlier memory decisions.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: The reset "ensures the agent cannot solve the final task via residual context", which forces write-then-retrieve.
- **Correctness / verification**: HotpotQA gold scored by an LLM judge. Auxiliary context-management and memory-quality terms are shaped.
- **Difficulty control**: Distractor volume, whether a reset happens, stage count, overflow penalties.
- **Reported results**: Trained only on HotpotQA. Average over ALFWorld, SciWorld, PDDL, BabyAI and HotpotQA: 54.31% on Qwen3-4B-Instruct (+8.57 pp over the best baseline, A-Mem) and 41.96% on Qwen2.5-7B (+4.82 over Mem0). RL adds about 8.5–8.7 pp over the no-RL variant.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Judge-scored task and memory quality are both gameable.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Any QA seed with supporting facts can be staged as facts → wipe + distractors → question at near-zero generation cost.

### UMA + Ledger-QA — Learning to Remember: End-to-End Training of Memory Agents for Long-Context Reasoning (Zhang et al., 2026)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18493
- **Mechanism**: A single policy builds a structured Memory Bank with CRUD operations once from a stream. The memory is reused across many future QA sessions it cannot anticipate; answers use the bank plus raw-context retrieval. Task-Stratified GRPO samples G=16 memory rollouts, branches each into M QA sessions, and normalizes memory advantages across memory rollouts and QA advantages only among sessions answering the same question. Ledger-QA samples H dates (2023–2024) and consumption intents. A strong LLM (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro) jointly generates natural dialogue and aligned transaction records, with amounts decided during this joint synthesis. Questions in 8 categories are then derived programmatically from the ledger and require multi-step aggregation.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: The answer (e.g., total spend) is a latent sum over scattered updates and appears in no single span. Difficulty grows with H.
- **Correctness / verification**: Exact answers are computed from the structured ledger. Correctness still depends on the dialogue matching the LLM-co-generated ledger.
- **Difficulty control**: Session horizon H (tested 2 → 500) and question category.
- **Reported results**: On Ledger-QA, UMA-Specialist scores 53.02 judge / 49.39 EM and UMA-Generalist 38.18 / 34.39. MemAgent scores 40.52 judge, but only under a per-question protocol. At H=500: UMA-Specialist 25.00, MemAgent 11.54, RAG 5.77. Across 13 TTL and AR benchmarks at a 16K budget, UMA-Generalist averages 75.86 vs Mem-α 63.02 and A-MEM 58.75.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Single domain (finance). Dialogue–ledger drift if alignment checks are weak. Low absolute accuracy at large H.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Keep ground-truth state as a program variable (inventory, calendar, repo state). Have an LLM render each update as dialogue, ideally with the program choosing values, and derive questions by code. Raise H when accuracy saturates.

### ReSum / SUPO / Context-Folding — ReSum: Unlocking Long-Horizon Search Intelligence via Context Summarization (Wu et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13313 · SUPO: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06727 · Context-Folding: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967
- **Mechanism**: The task stays the same while the interaction budget grows past the window. ReSum periodically summarizes and restarts from the summary. ReSum-GRPO splits trajectories at summary points and applies the final advantage to every segment. SUPO folds LLM-generated summarization into the policy and derives a policy gradient that standard RL infrastructure can optimize end to end. Context-Folding lets the agent branch into a sub-trajectory and fold it into an outcome summary, trained with FoldGRPO process rewards.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Seeds that fail because trajectories are truncated (the ReSum authors observe failed BrowseComp cases exceeding 32K) become solvable only with learned compression.
- **Correctness / verification**: Outcome verifiers are unchanged.
- **Difficulty control**: Maximum rounds or summaries, working-context budget, and test-time round scaling (SUPO).
- **Reported results**: ReSum is +4.5% over ReAct training-free and a further +8.2% with ReSum-GRPO, using 1K training samples. SUPO: +3.2% on CodeGym and +14.0% on BrowseComp-Plus (53%) at the same or smaller working context. Context-Folding: pass@1 0.620 on BrowseComp-Plus (+20%) and 0.580 on SWE-bench Verified (+8.8%), with a 10× smaller active context. 2026 follow-ups: MMPO keeps 97.1% of performance at 1.75M tokens by penalizing high belief-entropy summaries (2605.30159). CAMG uses an episode-persistent shell workspace, where files are the memory, across Shop/Coding/DeepResearch/AutoResearch (2609.34422). AdaCoM trains an external context manager for frozen agents (2605.30785).
- **Limitations / failure modes**: This is a budget, not new content: seeds must genuinely need long horizons. Recursive summaries drift, which is MMPO's motivation.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Take long-horizon seeds that currently hit the context limit, add summarize or fold actions, broadcast advantages across segments, and evaluate with round budgets beyond training.

### IterResearch *(added)* — IterResearch: Rethinking Long-Horizon Agents with Interaction Scaling (Chen et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07327
- **Mechanism**: MDP-style "workspace reconstruction". Each round the agent keeps an evolving report as memory plus the latest observation, instead of the full history. It is trained with EAPO: geometric reward discounting that favours shorter successful trajectories, plus adaptive downsampling for distributed stability.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: It lets the interaction budget, not the context, be the difficulty knob. Deep-research questions become solvable only with many more rounds.
- **Correctness / verification**: Benchmark answers unchanged.
- **Difficulty control**: T_max, the maximum number of interactions.
- **Reported results**: +14.5 pp average over open-source agents across six benchmarks. Trained with T_max=32, it scores 42.5% on BrowseComp-200 at T_max=2048, vs 15.2% at 32 (3.5% at T_max=2), within a 40K context. As a prompting paradigm it adds up to 19.2 pp over ReAct for frontier models.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Very long rollouts are expensive, and report memory can lose details.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: A strong "train short, test long" template. Train at a small round budget with a Markovian workspace and use T_max extrapolation as the held-out difficulty axis.

**Family B — sharded, underspecified and hidden-spec multi-turn tasks**

### Lost in Conversation (+ MultiChallenge) — LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation (Laban et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120 · MultiChallenge: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17399
- **Mechanism**: Fully specified instructions from six tasks (HumanEval/LiveCodeBench code, Spider SQL, BFCL actions, GSM8K math, ToTTo data-to-text, Summary of a Haystack) are sharded semi-automatically: LLM segmentation and rephrasing, then verification against the original, then manual inspection. Shards must satisfy P1 information preservation, P2 clear initial intent, P3 order insensitivity, P4 maximal sharding and P5 minimal transformation. A user simulator reveals at most one shard per turn. The assistant may attempt an answer at any time, and the original evaluator scores it. MultiChallenge covers instruction retention, inference memory, reliable versioned editing and self-coherence. Its items are LLM-generated, then human-reviewed, and kept only if at least 3 of 6 frontier models fail.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Same information, delivered piecemeal. Models commit early, over-rely on their own earlier wrong attempts, and lose track.
- **Correctness / verification**: CONCAT (all shards as bullets in one turn) averages 95.1% of FULL, which shows information is preserved. MultiChallenge uses instance-level rubrics: 93.95% agreement with human raters vs 37.33% for a baseline auto-evaluator.
- **Difficulty control**: Number and order of shards; RECAP and SNOWBALL (restating earlier shards) are mitigation baselines.
- **Reported results**: 600 sharded instructions, 15 LLMs, more than 200,000 simulated conversations. Performance drops 39% from FULL to SHARDED: aptitude −16%, unreliability +112%. Lowering temperature cuts unreliability 50–80% single-turn but only about 15–20% sharded, and about 30% unreliability remains at T=0. MultiChallenge's top model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, June 2024) averages 41.4%.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Preparing 90–120 sharded instructions took 1–4 hours of manual work per task. Results depend on the user simulator. "Intent Mismatch" (2602.07338) argues part of the gap is structural ambiguity rather than missing capability, and proposes a mediator that rewrites user turns.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Shard saturated single-turn RLVR seeds, reuse their verifiers, and run CONCAT on every item. Discard items where CONCAT fails. Data and code are released as Microsoft/lost_in_conversation.

### RLAAR — Mitigating Lost in Multi-turn Conversation via Curriculum RL with Verifiable Accuracy and Abstention Rewards (Li et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18731
- **Mechanism**: GSM8K and Eurus code problems (TACO, CodeContests, Codeforces, APPS) are split into up to K_max=5 shards and rolled out on-policy over turns. "Unsolvable-Multi" variants truncate after M<K shards, with gold action "Abstain". A curriculum first measures a single-turn baseline reward r̄₀. It then adds shards once mean reward exceeds ρ·r̄₀ (default ρ=0.8) and finally samples K uniformly in [1, K_max]. Abstention ratio m (default 0.1) sets the share of unsolvable rollouts.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Adds multi-turn integration plus a calibrated "enough information yet?" decision.
- **Correctness / verification**: Original answer match or unit tests. Abstain is rewarded only on truncated episodes.
- **Difficulty control**: K, ρ and m.
- **Reported results**: Qwen3-8B: LiC score 62.6% → 75.1%, abstain score 33.5% → 73.4%. Curriculum ablation on Qwen3-1.7B (baseline 58.8 LiC): no curriculum 63.2; ρ=1.0 65.6 (stuck, 1000+ steps to stage 3); ρ=0.9 72.2 (470 steps); ρ=0.8 71.9 (90 steps); ρ=0.7 62.1; ρ=0.6 40.9.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Truncation assumes every missing shard is necessary, so redundant shards mislabel "unsolvable" items. String-matched abstention invites always-abstain if m is too high. Only math and code were tested.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Gate shard count on competence relative to the single-turn baseline. Check shard necessity with a solver or ablation before labelling a truncation as unsolvable.

### Scalable sharding + memory-augmented RL (+ FiC, MAIGO) — Multi-Turn Reasoning When Context Arrives in Pieces: Scalable Sharding and Memory-Augmented RL (Luo et al., 2026)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12941 · FiC: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24432 · MAIGO: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27186
- **Mechanism**: A three-step prompting pipeline needs only 1–3 annotated few-shot examples: (1) segment the problem into minimal logical units; (2) verify completeness and non-redundancy; (3) extract the core question, withhold it from the shard sequence, and repeat it every turn. LongBench documents are cut into sentence-aligned chunks (10–14 shards on average). Training variants are Sharded, Full-Question (the full question in one random shard, Wikitext noise elsewhere) and Mixed (shards interleaved with noise). The policy keeps a free-text memory capped at 256 tokens and sees only question + memory + current shard. It is trained with dual-clip PPO and Dr.GRPO-style normalization, G=4. FiC ("view-asymmetric self-distillation") uses the same model on the single-turn CONCAT view as teacher and aligns the on-policy sharded student token by token. MAIGO distills from "history-cleaned" references that drop earlier assistant replies, and down-weights disagreeing middle turns.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Information arrives in pieces through a tight memory, with optional noise shards.
- **Correctness / verification**: A rule-based verifier checks GSM8K gold. Shard completeness is LLM-verified. FiC and MAIGO need no verifier for training: the teacher is the model's own fully specified view.
- **Difficulty control**: Shard count, noise shards (MATH500 test with n ∈ {2, 4} per turn), memory cap and response budget.
- **Reported results**: Base models lose 19.4–64.9 pp from single-turn to sharded. Memory RL beats full-history RL by 15.2–35.6 pp, even when both get full history at test. Qwen3-4B (1024-token responses), trained only on sharded GSM8K: GSM8K 0.370 → 0.877, MATH500 0.260 → 0.638, and 0.570 at n=4 noise with the Mixed variant. LongBench QA F1 rises 31.7–43.4%. With 512-token responses, thinking-model MATH500 collapses to 0.050. FiC recovers ≥92% of single-turn performance (100% on two Llama backbones). MAIGO: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct sharded accuracy 52.8 → 66.1, SHARDED/FULL 66.5% → 84.1%, FULL within 2.3 points.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: LLM-verified shards; training only on GSM8K; 256-token memory unsuitable for verbatim details; self-distillation is capped by single-turn ability.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: The easy view is a free teacher. Shard automatically, train with bounded memory, and add FiC/MAIGO self-distillation when RL rewards are too sparse. Mix noise shards in training (the Mixed variant was the most robust).

### CollabLLM — CollabLLM: From Passive Responders to Active Collaborators (Wu et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00640
- **Mechanism**: Single-turn datasets become collaborations with a user simulator that holds the full target and reveals it gradually. MediumDocEdit-Chat uses 100 Medium articles summarized into target problems (BLEU against the article). BigCodeBench-Chat uses 600 problems (pass rate). MATH-Chat uses 200 level-5 MATH problems (accuracy). The multiturn-aware reward samples future continuations with the simulator (w=2 is the default trade-off) and scores extrinsic task success plus intrinsic −min(λ·tokens, 1) plus an LLM-judged interactivity term. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is trained with LoRA via SFT, offline/online DPO and PPO.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: The agent must elicit intent before producing the artifact.
- **Correctness / verification**: Final-artifact metrics against the hidden target.
- **Difficulty control**: How much the simulator withholds, simulator persona, window w.
- **Reported results**: +18.5% task performance and +46.3% interactivity (LLM judges) on average vs the best baselines. In a 201-person user study, satisfaction rose 17.6% and time spent fell 10.4%.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Open-source simulators "often get confused" and start solving the task as the assistant. Interactivity is judge-based. Forward sampling costs compute.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Wrap any verifiable seed in a simulator that knows the full spec. Score turns by short forward rollouts plus the seed verifier on the resulting artifact.

### UserRL / UserBench — UserRL: Training Interactive User-Centric Agent via Reinforcement Learning (Qian et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19736 · UserBench: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22034
- **Mechanism**: Eight gyms each hide information in a simulated user: Intention, Turtle (lateral-thinking story), Telepathy (target entity), Function (hidden arithmetic rule), Travel (UserBench preferences revealed gradually), Tau (tau-bench wrapper), Persuade and Search. GRPO uses turn-level reward reshaping (Naive, Equalized, Reward-to-Go, Exponential Mapping) and trajectory scoring (Sum, R2G). All RL models start from an SFT cold start, with Qwen3-32B as the training simulator, 16 turns max and no KL.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: The key parameters of a task are moved into another agent that must be interrogated.
- **Correctness / verification**: Deterministic, rule-based transitions in several gyms (e.g., FunctionGym applies the hidden rule). Others use LLM evaluation.
- **Difficulty control**: Amount of hidden information, simulator strength, turn budget.
- **Reported results**: Qwen3-8B with Equalized/R2G reaches a gym average of 0.5652, vs GPT-4o 0.4449 and Gemini-2.5-Pro 0.4702. The average mixes metric scales; IntentionGym exceeds 1. Naive sparse rewards collapse training. SFT cold start gives more than 100% gains on some gyms. Training with the Qwen3-32B simulator transfers to GPT-4o evaluation, while stronger simulators train faster. On UserBench, answers fully align with all user intents only 20% of the time, and the best models elicit fewer than 30% of preferences.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Judge-scored gyms; co-adaptation to simulator quirks.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Start with the rule-verified gyms. For your domain, move a seed's parameters into a simulated user and reward only programmatically checkable final choices.

### InfoPO *(added)* — InfoPO: Information-Driven Policy Optimization for User-Centric Agents (Kong et al., 2026)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00656
- **Mechanism**: Multi-turn interaction is framed as uncertainty reduction. The per-turn information-gain reward compares the agent's next-action distribution, with teacher forcing on the realized next action, given the actual user feedback vs a masked "No information found." counterfactual. A variance-gated fusion combines it with the task outcome, so info gain matters most when outcome rewards have no within-group variance.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: None by itself; it is the credit-assignment piece that makes underspecified seeds learnable when trajectory rewards give zero GRPO advantage.
- **Correctness / verification**: Task outcome rewards from UserGym (UserRL's gyms), ColBench and tau2-bench. The info-gain term needs no labels.
- **Difficulty control**: Inherits the environments' hidden-information knobs.
- **Reported results**: Exceeds GRPO-based methods by 14–16%. The paper reports robustness under user-simulator shifts and transfer to environment-interaction tasks.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Info gain rewards any feedback that changes behaviour, not only correct changes; the outcome fusion is what anchors it.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Add the masked-feedback counterfactual as a dense bonus whenever sharded or hidden-spec groups show zero reward variance.

### CLARITI — Asking What Matters: Reward-Driven Clarification for Software Engineering Tasks (Vijayvargiya et al., 2026)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14624
- **Mechanism**: SWE-bench Verified issues are coded with a six-category codebook: Error Info, Implementation Details, Version/Environment, Reproduction, Expected Behavior, External References. For each of 500 issues, GPT-5 writes 3 rewrites omitting randomly chosen subsets of the present categories, giving 1,500 variants. A GPT-5 simulated user answers questions with access to the full issue. OpenHands with Seed-OSS-36B solves the task after a single clarification round. Shapley attribution over categories gives the impact weights. An 8B clarification module is trained with GRPO on staged rubric rewards: non-redundancy, diversity, answerability, and category-weighted task relevance.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Removes exactly the information types that matter, and the Shapley analysis measures how much each matters.
- **Correctness / verification**: Hidden tests unchanged. 50 random rewrites were manually checked to omit the target categories and keep the others.
- **Difficulty control**: Which and how many categories are removed.
- **Reported results**: On a 700-instance sample, resolution falls from 43.8% (full) to 23.7% (underspecified). On the clarification evaluation, CLARITI scores 36.8% with 3.0 questions vs GPT-5's 5.1 (41% fewer), about 88% of the fully specified 41.6%. Shapley weights: Error Info 0.340, Implementation Details 0.184, Version/Env 0.163, Reproduction 0.143, Expected Behavior 0.105, External References 0.065.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: The rewriter and simulator are GPT-5 (leakage risk). Only a sample was validated. Single-round setting.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: For spec-plus-hidden-tests seeds, write category-targeted removal rewrites. Estimate each category's impact (Shapley or ablation) and weight the reward for asking about it accordingly.

### QuestBench (+ AskBench, CIGAsk, STaR-GATE) — QuestBench: Can LLMs ask the right question to acquire information in reasoning tasks? (Li et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22674 · AskBench: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11199 · CIGAsk: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24290 · STaR-GATE: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19154
- **Mechanism**: Underspecification is formalized as a CSP missing variable assignments. "1-sufficient" problems are built by removing exactly one necessary assignment: Logic-Q (propositional), Planning-Q (PDDL with a partially observed initial state), GSM-Q (human-annotated GSM with one unknown) and GSME-Q (equation form). The model picks the question that makes the problem solvable, as multiple choice. AskBench converts QA pairs into multi-turn checkpointed interactions, with intent-deficient (AskMind) and false-premise (AskOverconfidence) settings, trained with rubric-guided RLVR. CIGAsk runs multi-turn GRPO with Counterfactual Information Gain: gold-answer log-likelihood under a frozen reference with vs without the user's reply ("how to ask"). An Asymmetric Ambiguity Bonus uses the gold ambiguity label ("when to ask"). STaR-GATE iteratively fine-tunes a Questioner on questions that raise an oracle's probability of the gold personalized response. It uses 25,500 persona-task prompts with a Roleplayer that has latent preferences.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Solving well-specified problems does not imply knowing what is missing.
- **Correctness / verification**: A solver computes the set of sufficient questions, so the gold is exact. CIGAsk uses gold answers and ambiguity labels.
- **Difficulty control**: Problem size and search depth; domain (logic and planning are much harder than GSM).
- **Reported results**: Models do well on GSM-Q and GSME-Q but reach only 40–50% on Logic-Q and Planning-Q, even when they solve the fully specified versions. CIGAsk reports that prompting alone yields models that ask on every query or ask vague questions. STaR-GATE responses are preferred over the initial model's on 72% of tasks after two iterations.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Multiple-choice format, a single missing variable, synthetic domains.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: For procedurally generated seeds, delete one necessary fact and let the solver certify which question restores solvability. That gives an exact "ask" target at any size.

### Future-turn clarification preferences *(added)* — Modeling Future Conversation Turns to Teach LLMs to Ask Clarifying Questions (Zhang et al., 2024)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13788
- **Mechanism**: For ambiguous open-domain questions with several annotated interpretations, each interpretation is a simulated user. Candidate first responses (a direct answer vs a clarifying question) are labelled by simulating the next turn for every user and checking whether each gets their own gold answer ("double-turn" preferences). Preference training on these labels teaches both when to ask and what to ask.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: A single-answer QA seed becomes a multi-user task. Answering one interpretation fails the others.
- **Correctness / verification**: Per-interpretation gold answers from multi-annotation QA datasets; F1 against the answer set.
- **Difficulty control**: Degree of ambiguity (number of interpretations); share of unambiguous items, which must be answered directly.
- **Reported results**: +5% F1 against the answer set across interpretations, and +3% accuracy on the ask-or-answer decision vs prior-context-only labelling.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Needs datasets with enumerated interpretations; a simulated user answers from a fixed interpretation.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Where a seed admits several valid parameterizations, enumerate them as simulated users, label preferences by next-turn outcomes, and mix in unambiguous items so the model does not learn to always ask.

### SWEET-RL / ColBench — SWEET-RL: Training Multi-Turn LLM Agents on Collaborative Reasoning Tasks (Zhou et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15478
- **Mechanism**: Backend tasks: Llama-3.1-70B writes a Python function inspired by a DCLM web excerpt plus unit tests, and only functions that pass their own tests are kept (10K training tasks). Frontend tasks: WebSight pages (10K train; 500 manually inspected test). The agent gets a high-level description, and a simulated human who can see the reference answers questions for up to 10 rounds. SWEET-RL trains a turn-wise advantage model (Bradley–Terry over trajectory pairs, parameterized as a normalized log-ratio) that sees the reference as training-time privileged information. The policy is optimized per turn with DPO: 16 candidates, top-50% vs bottom-50%.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Information asymmetry. Single-turn attempts from the description alone mostly fail.
- **Correctness / verification**: Success = all 10 hidden unit tests pass; CLIP cosine similarity for frontend.
- **Difficulty control**: Description detail and round budget.
- **Reported results**: Backend success, single-turn vs collaborative: Llama-3.1-8B 6.9% vs 22.4% (zero-shot); GPT-4o 16.2% vs 40.4%. With Llama-3.1-8B, SWEET-RL reaches 40.4% backend success (multi-turn DPO 34.4%) and a 48.2% frontend win rate vs GPT-4o (42.8%), about +6% absolute, matching GPT-4o's 40.4%.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: The simulator may leak code or be unhelpful. CLIP is coarse.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Any seed with a reference solution and hidden tests becomes collaborative. Give a lossy description and put the reference in the simulator and critic, never in the policy.

**Family C — cooperative, partial-information and multi-agent tasks**

### Sotopia-RL — Sotopia-RL: Reward Design for Social Intelligence (Yu et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03905
- **Mechanism**: GPT-4o self-plays 100 Sotopia scenarios, each with two private and partly conflicting goals. GPT-4o then attributes each episode's outcome to individual utterances offline, using the full episode, along goal, relationship and knowledge-seeking dimensions. A reward model trained on these labels drives single-turn online GRPO (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) after behaviour cloning.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Partial information and a partner with opposed interests.
- **Correctness / verification**: LLM-judged, not programmatic. The multi-dimensional rewards are meant to reduce hacking.
- **Difficulty control**: Scenario subset (Sotopia-hard = 14 scenarios) and partner model.
- **Reported results**: With GPT-4o as partner, goal completion is 7.17 on Sotopia-hard and 8.31 on Sotopia-all; GPT-4o itself scores 6.97. With a BC partner: BC 6.76, Sotopia-π 6.84, goal-only RL 7.21, full Sotopia-RL 7.81. Offline attribution (7.81) beats online attribution.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Judge dependence; small scenario pool; no automatic partner escalation.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: A reference for partial-information social tasks. Prefer rule-decided outcomes where possible: Social Gym (2608.09128) has 21 multi-agent games with rule-decided outcomes, and MINDGAMES (2605.29512) releases 29,571 games.

### MAPoRL (+ Coral, MAGRPO) — MAPoRL: Multi-Agent Post-Co-Training for Collaborative Large Language Models with Reinforcement Learning (Park et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18439 · Coral: https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/collaborative-reasoner-self-improving-social-agents-with-synthetic-conversations/ · MAGRPO: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04652
- **Mechanism**: Agents answer GSM8K/TinyGSM or ANLI items independently, then debate over turns. The reward is a **learned verifier's** score: a quantized Phi-3-mini (or Qwen2.5-3B / Llama-3-8B) with a sigmoid head, trained on correctness-labelled solutions. Each agent is credited with the verifier scores of the responses it influenced ("influence-aware"), with optional collaboration-incentive shaping. Coral (Meta FAIR) turns reasoning problems into two-agent conversations where success requires disagreeing with incorrect solutions, convincing the partner and agreeing on a final answer. Matrix-generated self-play conversations are filtered to turns that convince the partner of the correct solution, and used for SFT and DPO. MAGRPO (Liu et al., "LLM Collaboration With Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning") applies group-relative advantages to cooperative writing and coding.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: The difficulty moves into persuasion and correction of a partner who may be wrong.
- **Correctness / verification**: MAPoRL relies on a learned verifier trained from gold labels, which can be hacked (the paper has a reward-hacking section). Coral's metric is agreement correctness against gold.
- **Difficulty control**: Number of agents and turns, partner quality, co-training vs a fixed partner.
- **Reported results**: Off-the-shelf LLMs do not improve through vanilla collaboration while MAPoRL-trained ones do. SFT on high-quality collaboration samples does not induce collaborative behaviour. Coral: up to 29.4% over single-agent chain-of-thought on general, math, science and social reasoning tasks.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Seeds a single agent already solves give little signal. Agreement rewards invite sycophancy and collusion. Compute grows with the number of agents.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Plant a partner with a confident, plausible wrong answer (known by construction) and reward only a gold-correct final consensus.

### MARSHAL (+ Among Us, Werewolf) — MARSHAL: Incentivizing Multi-Agent Reasoning via Self-Play with Strategic LLMs (Yuan et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15414 · Among Us: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06060 · Werewolf: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18940
- **Mechanism**: Qwen3-4B self-plays Tic-Tac-Toe, Kuhn Poker and cooperative Mini Hanabi. Held-out evaluation uses larger versions: Connect Four, Leduc Hold'em, Simple Hanabi. Credit assignment uses a turn-level "sum-then-normalize" advantage with agent-specific normalization. The Among Us work rewards "listening" (predicting the imposter from discussion) and "speaking" (influence on other agents' beliefs) as dense signals. The Werewolf agent has an LLM generate diverse candidate actions and an RL policy choose among them, to counter the LLM's intrinsic action bias.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Hidden information plus the need to model other agents; game size scales difficulty.
- **Correctness / verification**: Game engines and hidden roles or cards fixed by the environment.
- **Difficulty control**: Game size (mini → simple), self-play partners.
- **Reported results**: MARSHAL generalist: +28.7% on Leduc Hold'em and +22.9% on Simple Hanabi. Inside AutoGen and MAD multi-agent systems: up to +10.0% AIME, +7.6% GPQA-Diamond, +3.5% on average. Swapping in SPIRAL's role-conditioned advantage drops Mini Hanabi from 54.33% to 24.93% and Simple Hanabi from 36.75% to 5.53%. A Kuhn-Poker specialist mode-collapses on non-poker games. Among Us: win rates double vs standard RL. Werewolf agents reach human-level performance in human-agent games.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Toy games. Partners are copies of the policy, so there is no ad-hoc teamwork.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Add cooperative imperfect-information games at small size to the RL mix, use turn-level advantages, and grow the game once scores saturate. SpyRL/RLSVR (note 17) applies the same verifiable-hidden-role idea to open-ended tasks.

### SPAG *(added)* — Self-playing Adversarial Language Game Enhances LLM Reasoning (Cheng et al., 2024)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10642
- **Mechanism**: In Adversarial Taboo, an attacker who alone knows a target word (drawn from a 50K top-frequency vocabulary) tries to make the defender say it unknowingly. The defender tries to infer and name it. If nobody wins within T₀ turns, it is a tie. Models are first imitation-trained on GPT-4 game episodes, then iteratively self-play with a copy of themselves and update by offline RL on game outcomes (ReST-style selection with an advantage-weighted loss).
- **How it makes tasks harder**: A simple vocabulary item becomes a hidden-information, zero-sum dialogue requiring inference and strategic phrasing.
- **Correctness / verification**: Outcome is judged by a rule: whether the target word appears in the defender's utterance, or the defender's guess is correct.
- **Difficulty control**: Target-word pool, turn limit, self-play epochs.
- **Reported results**: Reasoning benchmarks improve uniformly across the tested open LLMs and keep improving over SPAG epochs (qualitative summary; per-benchmark numbers are in the paper).
- **Limitations / failure modes**: The game itself saturates. Transfer depends on the imitation warm-start.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Any entity or fact seed can become a Taboo-style hidden-target game with a string-match verifier. This is a cheap way to generate adversarial multi-turn data from a vocabulary list.

**Family D — adversarial content in environments**

### AgentDojo — AgentDojo: A Dynamic Environment to Evaluate Prompt Injection Attacks and Defenses for LLM Agents (Debenedetti et al., 2024)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352
- **Mechanism**: Four environments (Workspace, Slack, Travel, e-banking) hold mutable state, with injection placeholders inside tool-returned data. Every user task has a deterministic utility function over the final state, and every injection task a deterministic security function. Security cases are the user × injection cross product. Ground-truth call sequences show which placeholders the agent will actually read.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: The same user task must be completed while untrusted data carries a competing instruction.
- **Correctness / verification**: State-based utility and security checks, with no judge. Injected content never changes what the user task requires.
- **Difficulty control**: Attack template, injection position (end of tool output is most effective), injection target, defenses, adaptive attackers.
- **Reported results**: 97 user tasks, 629 security cases, 27 injection targets. The text says 74 tools and Table 1 says 70. GPT-4o: 69.0% benign utility. Targeted ASR with "important message" is 57.7%, vs 5.41% for "ignore previous" and 3.66% for TODO. End-of-response injections reach up to 70% average success. A tool filter lowers ASR to 7.5%. Most models lose 10–25 points of absolute utility under attack, and more capable models tend to be easier to attack.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Small task pool. Static templates saturate, and defenses look better than they are against adaptive attacks.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Give every verifiable tool environment utility and security functions. Inject attacker text into designated tool outputs on a share of rollouts, and reward utility AND security, both from final state.

### Adaptive Attacks Break Defenses *(added)* — Adaptive Attacks Break Defenses Against Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks on LLM Agents (Zhan et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00061
- **Mechanism**: A white-box attacker who knows the agent and its defense adds an optimized adversarial suffix or prefix to the injected instruction inside external content. Methods: GCG targeting the attacker-tool call, multi-objective GCG that also fools a detector, two-stage variants, and AutoDAN. Evaluated on InjecAgent with a Vicuna-7B prompted agent and a Llama3-8B fine-tuned agent.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Attack strength is optimized against the current defense instead of fixed templates.
- **Correctness / verification**: Success = the agent invokes the attacker tool (programmatic).
- **Difficulty control**: Optimizer, number of steps, objectives (stealth vs execution).
- **Reported results**: All eight evaluated defenses are bypassed, with ASR consistently above 50%, often exceeding the undefended ASR.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: White-box; tested on older open models.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Keep a defense-aware adaptive attacker as a held-out evaluator (and optionally a training adversary) for any injection operator. Low static ASR is not evidence of robustness.

### Instruction Hierarchy → IH-Challenge — IH-Challenge: A Training Dataset to Improve Instruction Hierarchy on Frontier LLMs (Guo et al., 2026)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10521 · The Instruction Hierarchy: Training LLMs to Prioritize Privileged Instructions (Wallace et al., 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13208
- **Mechanism**: 2024 (GPT-3.5 Turbo, SFT + RLHF). "Context synthesis" splits a compositional aligned instruction (e.g., "write a 20 line poem in spanish") across privilege levels, with the target being the full-instruction response. "Context ignorance" injects misaligned instructions into lower-privilege turns or tool outputs, with the target being the response as if they were absent. 2026: each task skeleton holds high-priority instructions, a placeholder for a conflicting low-priority message, and a per-datapoint Python grader. During RL on GPT-5-Mini, a **frozen**, guardrail-free attacker LLM probes the current defender through an evaluation tool and fills the placeholder online. Splits: single-constraint, multi-constraint, input-conditioned and anti-over-refusal.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: A trivially easy instruction becomes a robustness task under an adaptive adversary.
- **Correctness / verification**: The grader depends only on the high-priority instruction, so any low-priority filler leaves the label intact. Tasks are deliberately "IF-simple", so failures reflect hierarchy rather than instruction-following, and the attacker cannot win by making tasks hard to follow.
- **Difficulty control**: Attacker budget and iterations, number of constraints, split mix.
- **Reported results**: 2024: +63% on system-prompt extraction and more than +30% on jailbreaks, with some over-refusal regressions. 2026: IH robustness 84.1% → 94.1% averaged over 16 benchmarks, unsafe behaviour 6.6% → 0.7%, and the internal static agentic PI benchmark 0.44 → 1.00. Dropping the anti-over-refusal split: over-refusal 0.950 → 0.831 and helpfulness 0.773 → 0.613. Dataset: huggingface.co/datasets/openai/ih-challenge.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: The attacker pipeline is not released. IF-simple tasks limit capability co-training. Static PI evals saturate.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Wrap programmatically graded IF seeds with a conflict slot filled online, and always include compatible-instruction twins.

### SecAlign → Meta-SecAlign — Meta-SecAlign: Training LLMs against Prompt Injection for Robust Agents (Chen et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02735 · SecAlign: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05451
- **Mechanism**: Starting from generic instruction data, another sample's instruction is injected into the untrusted data part (a dedicated "input" role). DPO prefers the answer to the legitimate instruction over the answer to the injection. Meta-SecAlign places injections at the end (45%) or start (45%) of the data rather than always at the end, and uses the model's own generations as labels.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Any instruction seed becomes adversarial at zero generation cost.
- **Correctness / verification**: The secure target is the response to the original instruction; the insecure target is the response to the injection.
- **Difficulty control**: Injection position and source; GCG/adaptive attacks at evaluation.
- **Reported results**: SecAlign pushed various injection ASRs below 10%, but always-at-end injections taught a shortcut ("ignore the last instruction"), and AgentDojo utility collapsed to 6.2% vs 59.8% undefended. Randomizing position alone raises AgentDojo utility by 69.0 pp. Meta-SecAlign-70B on AgentDojo: utility 84.5%, ASR 1.9%. Undefended Llama-3.3-70B: 59.8% utility, 14.7% ASR. InjecAgent ASR 53.8% → 0.5%; WASP 2.4% → 0.0%. AutoInject's RL suffixes still break Meta-SecAlign-70B.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Offline, static injections; adaptive optimizers still succeed.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Cross-inject seeds into each other's data fields, randomize position, label with the policy's own outputs, and add online attackers later.

### Self-RedTeam (+ MART) — Chasing Moving Targets with Online Self-Play Reinforcement Learning for Safer Language Models (Liu et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07468 · MART: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07689
- **Mechanism**: One policy with shared parameters plays attacker and defender in a zero-sum game, both with hidden chain-of-thought. The attacker rewrites WildJailbreak seeds (26,000, 50:50 vanilla harmful and vanilla benign) into stealthier harmful or deceptive-looking benign prompts. A revision-faithfulness sub-reward pays the attacker only if the seed's harmful or benign intent is preserved. WildGuard-7B labels prompt harm, response harm and refusal, with Qwen3Guard as a robustness check. An auxiliary self-distilled SFT loss preserves general ability. MART (2023) is the offline predecessor: adversarial and target LLMs alternate rounds.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Attack difficulty tracks the current defender automatically.
- **Correctness / verification**: Seed labels fixed by construction and enforced by the faithfulness reward; guard-model judges cross-checked.
- **Difficulty control**: Adaptive through co-evolution; benign/harmful mix; hidden CoT on or off.
- **Reported results**: Average relative harmful-refusal improvement: 33.4% (Qwen2.5-3B), 39.5% (7B), 44.7% (14B), 45.2% (Llama3.1-8B). ASR falls up to 95% for RLHF-aligned models across 14 benchmarks. Attacks are 17.8% more diverse (SBERT). An attacker trained alone against a fixed defender collapses onto one tactic 86.3% of the time. Without hidden CoT, Llama over-refuses (XSTest compliance 0.528, restored to 0.936). Self-play costs about 44–48% more compute. Llama shows modest AlpacaEval-2 degradation, and shared-parameter Llama attackers sometimes refuse to attack. MART cuts violation rate up to 84.7% after 4 rounds.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Judge-model dependence; attacker refusal; capability regression on some families.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Pair each harmful seed with a benign lookalike, let an online attacker rewrite both under a class-preservation reward, and reward the defender for the correct refuse-or-comply decision on both.

### ARLAS (+ CoER) — Adversarial Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Model Agent Safety (Wang et al., 2025)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05442 · CoER: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07529
- **Mechanism**: ARLAS warms up an attacker and an agent by imitation on 10K teacher episodes, then co-trains them with GRPO in a zero-sum game on BrowserGym. The attacker's injection is inserted at a random line of the page. The agent must complete the task without leaking user information. The agent trains against **all previous attacker checkpoints** to avoid cyclic learning, while the attacker trains against the current agent. CoER models adaptive IPI as a general-sum Markov game in which the attacker can inject multiple times and adapt to the defender's responses. Its environment is derived from AgentDyn across seven domains, with deterministic task and attack verifiers, candidate injection sites and executable attack contracts. The attacker is SFT-initialized from successful DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Seed2.0 attacks. Co-PPO keeps historical populations for both roles. Learned attackers then challenge teacher agents, and only demonstrations verified as safe AND task-complete fine-tune the defender (Qwen3.5-9B).
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Capability tasks become adversarial, and attacker strength rises automatically.
- **Correctness / verification**: Programmatic task-success and leakage/attack checks; the attacker controls only observation text or designated sites.
- **Difficulty control**: Training iterations, population sampling, number of injections per episode (CoER).
- **Reported results**: ARLAS on AgentDojo (Gemma3-12B): ASR 6.3% → 5.4% and task success 24.4% → 25.8%, on par with SPAG (5.4 / 26.2). Variants without the population fail to reduce ASR. CoER (seven-domain eval, 1,512 executions): overall attack success 38.5% → 0.2%, utility 63.2% → 76.3%. The main eval uses CoER's own attackers.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Small AgentDojo gains for ARLAS; sparse rewards; self-referential evaluation in CoER.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Add an attacker restricted to untrusted observation slots in existing agentic RL environments. Train the agent against a pool of past attackers, and distil only doubly verified (safe and successful) demonstrations.

### GPT-Red (+ Gemini adaptive adversarial training) — GPT-Red: Automated Red Teaming via Self-Play at Scale (Wallace et al., 2026)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26115 · Lessons from Defending Gemini Against Indirect Prompt Injections (Shi et al., 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14534
- **Mechanism**: OpenAI converts existing capability tasks into adversarial environments. Mid-rollout, the attacker may edit only a designated section (a tool response, a local-file region, a constrained web-page region for computer use, or a rendered image produced via an image API). The edit is placed at a pre-specified position. Programmatic and LLM checks reject invalid attacks (schema or length constraints). GPT-Red is rewarded for valid failures. A **population** of simultaneously trained defenders is rewarded for resisting AND completing the original task. The attacks then adversarially train GPT-5.6. Gemini's work builds synthetic function-calling conversations with private data. Adaptive attacks (Actor-Critic, Beam Search, TAP, Linear Generation) generate triggers in emails or calendar events. Corrective responses are synthesized, filtered with a user-instruction classifier, and used for SFT; adversarial RL is applied as well.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: The defender faces an RL-trained adversary that discovers new attack classes. The paper highlights "fake chain-of-thought" injections that mimic the defender's own reasoning.
- **Correctness / verification**: Threat-model enforcement keeps the user task well-defined. Invalid attacks earn nothing. Defender reward includes original-task completion.
- **Difficulty control**: Adaptive by construction; defender-population diversity.
- **Reported results**: GPT-Red finds more successful attacks than human red-teamers on 2025-Q4 Indirect Prompt Injection Challenge scenarios, and breaks past models up to GPT-5.5. GPT-5.6's robustness to fake chain-of-thought attacks rises from 5.2% to 95.9%. Held-out IPI red-teaming ASR is below 4%. Held-out strong-attack robustness exceeds 50% (up to 89%). Attackers trained against a single defender generalize worse and mode-collapse. Described as the single largest documented LLM safety training run. Gemini: TAP ASR on the email/passport scenario is 99.8% for Gemini 2.0 vs 53.6% for 2.5. In 16 of 24 defense×attack cases the adaptive attack equals or beats the non-adaptive one. Better instruction-followers were sometimes easier to attack.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Proprietary environments and models; very large compute; partly LLM-checked validity.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: An open-scale version: tag untrusted slots in your capability environments, train a small attacker against a pool of defender checkpoints, give zero reward to threat-model-violating attacks, and keep an adaptive attacker held out.

### WildTeaming (+ X-Teaming, ActorBreaker) — WildTeaming at Scale: From In-the-Wild Jailbreaks to (Adversarially) Safer Language Models (Jiang et al., 2024)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18510 · X-Teaming: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13203 · ActorBreaker/ActorAttack: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10700
- **Mechanism**: WildTeaming mines 105K human jailbreak tactics (5.7K clusters) from LMSYS-1M and WildChat. It composes 2–7 sampled tactics per attack with Mixtral-8×7B and GPT-4, then prunes off-topic and low-risk candidates. WildJailbreak (262K) has vanilla harmful 50,050, vanilla benign 50,050, adversarial harmful 82,728 (paired with refusals) and adversarial benign 78,706. X-Teaming plans persona, context and a turn-by-turn trajectory from neutral topics to the target, verifies and optimizes turns (TextGrad), and releases XGuard-Train: 30K multi-turn conversations from 10K WildJailbreak behaviours across 13 risk categories. ActorBreaker uses actor-network theory to find semantically related "actors" that lead gradually to unsafe content, and releases a multi-turn safety dataset.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Composition and multi-turn escalation hide intent. This is the safety mirror of benign sharding.
- **Correctness / verification**: Intent-preservation filters and verifier scores; refusals as targets for harmful items and helpful completions for benign twins.
- **Difficulty control**: Number and type of tactics, turns and escalation plans, optimizer iterations.
- **Reported results**: WildTeaming finds up to 4.6× more diverse successful attacks with 40% fewer attempts. Robust safeguards needed substantially more vanilla and adversarial data (up to 60K). X-Teaming reaches up to 98.1% ASR and 96.2% on Claude 3.7 Sonnet. XGuard-Train-tuned models have 52.2% X-Teaming ASR vs 93.7% (SafeMTData) and 80.5% (TuluMix-only), but are weaker against ActorAttack (18.9% vs 8.9%).
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Static data gets overtaken by adaptive attackers; classifier-based intent checks.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Generate harmful multi-turn decompositions and benign sharded twins from the same seed pool as contrastive pairs (see STAC in note 06 for tool-chain analogues).

### OR-Bench (+ FalseReject) — OR-Bench: An Over-Refusal Benchmark for Large Language Models (Cui et al., 2024)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20947 · FalseReject: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08054
- **Mechanism**: Mixtral-8×7B generates toxic seeds per category (e.g., 2,000) and rewrites each into five borderline-but-benign prompts. The rewrites avoid explicit safety terms and fictional framing. An ensemble of GPT-4-turbo, Llama-3-70B and Gemini-1.5-pro keeps prompts judged safe by majority. OR-Bench-Hard-1K keeps safe prompts rejected by at least 3 of the largest or newest models in each family. FalseReject builds 16K seemingly toxic queries across 44 categories with a graph-informed adversarial multi-agent framework, with structured-reasoning responses for SFT.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: Surface features that trigger refusal are kept while harm is removed; hardness is selected by how many models refuse.
- **Correctness / verification**: Majority vote of the LLM ensemble (over 98% of human-expert performance), plus a 600-prompt toxic control set.
- **Difficulty control**: Rejection-count threshold, category, rewrite constraints.
- **Reported results**: 80K over-refusal prompts, about 1K hard and 600 toxic, across 32 models. Spearman correlation between toxic-rejection and over-refusal rates is 0.89. FalseReject SFT reduces unnecessary refusals without hurting safety or general ability.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: LLM-certified benignness; single-turn, non-agentic.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: For every adversarial operator, also generate hardness-filtered benign twins from the same seeds.

### BENCH2ROBUST — Retry, Switch, or Abstain? Learning Strategy-Aware Tool-Use Policies via Controlled Error Injection (Chen et al., 2026)
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11977
- **Mechanism**: An adapter between the agent and tools injects failures. P(clean)=0.60 per call. Nine types: timeout, rate_limit, server_error, malformed, auth_error and schema drift (observable), plus factual_error (±10–25% value perturbation), stale and partial (silent). Each episode also gets a solvability scenario. S1: retry works. S2: one side of a fallback-equivalence class is blocked episode-wide, so the agent must switch tools; Retail gets 5 alternative tools, 21 in total. S3: all viable paths blocked, so the agent must stop and escalate. Stochastic injections are capped at B≤5 per episode and at most 2 consecutive failures per tool, separate from deterministic blocking. Five curriculum phases: 100% S1 → 20–40% S2 → +10% S3 → final 30/45/25. They auto-advance on mastery (e.g., Phase 4 requires >50% S3 transfer, <15 average turns, no S1/S2 regression). DAPO RL is used, with Bayesian Tool Memory (BTM) as an inference-time aid.
- **How it makes tasks harder**: A failure-free benchmark becomes one requiring retry, switch or stop decisions, including silent corruptions.
- **Correctness / verification**: Blocking is deterministic. S1/S2 are scored by the original evaluators. S3 is unsolvable by construction, but the reward has **no positive term for correct abstention**, and S3 is excluded from held-out success.
- **Difficulty control**: Failure probability and type mix, injection budget, scenario mix per phase.
- **Reported results**: 69 of 70 (model, subset) pairs degrade, by 1.8–46.7 pp. Qwen3-235B loses 15.8 pp on tau2-retail. Held-out Retail (402 tasks): BTM +16.8 pp without retraining; RL +6.3–6.9 pp without BTM; RL+BTM 40.8–45.5% under injection, with clean performance 63.9±1.3% vs base 64.3±0.9%. Without the partial-credit reward term, 38% of training tasks have zero reward variance.
- **Limitations / failure modes**: Abstention is not credited by existing evaluators, so the paper does not claim learned abstention. Silent corruptions need checks against the uncorrupted state.
- **How to reuse with easy seed tasks**: Wrap verifiable tool environments in a fault injector, assign a solvability scenario per episode, and add an explicit reward for stopping on S3. This complements the oracle-preserving noise in COVERT/RODS (note 06).

## Complexification operators from this area

1. **Objective stacking under bounded memory.** Packs k independent verifiable items into one episode with a fixed memory or turn budget.
   - *Easy → hard:* one HotpotQA question (Qwen2.5-14B ≈37% EM per item at 2 objectives) → 16 interleaved questions (≈3.5% per item). MEM1-7B trained at 2 reaches EM-sum 1.97.
   - *Keep verifiable:* per-item gold unchanged; require answer count = question count; remove closed-book-answerable items; compute chained answers by code if items are chained.
   - *Sources:* MEM1, MemAct.
2. **Distractor padding with chunked streaming.** Buries gold evidence in same-distribution distractors beyond the window and streams it through a fixed-size overwrite memory.
   - *Easy → hard:* HotpotQA with 2 gold paragraphs → the same question at 32K–60K training length and 3.5M test length, in 5K chunks with a 1,024-token memory (71.09% at 3.5M).
   - *Keep verifiable:* drop items solved without context (≈50% at MemAgent); string-check distractors for the gold.
   - *Sources:* MemAgent, Mem-α/MemoryAgentBench, LongMemEval (reading-only variants in note 07).
3. **Session-horizon accumulation, knowledge updates and context resets.** Spreads a latent state over H sessions of updates, or stages facts → wipe + distractors → query.
   - *Easy → hard:* "total spent" over 2 sessions → 500 sessions (Ledger-QA: UMA-Specialist 25.00, MemAgent 11.54). Or a newest-fact question over serial-numbered contradicting MQuAKE pairs at 262K tokens.
   - *Keep verifiable:* hold state in code and derive answers programmatically; order updates explicitly; use false-premise variants with abstention gold.
   - *Sources:* UMA/Ledger-QA, FactConsolidation, LongMemEval, AgeMem, Memory-R1.
4. **Interaction-budget extension with learned compression.** Same task, more rounds than fit in context, plus summarize, fold or workspace-rebuild actions.
   - *Easy → hard:* BrowseComp at T_max=32 (15.2%) → T_max=2048 (42.5%) with a report-as-memory workspace. Or SWE/deep-research with a 10× smaller active context (Context-Folding).
   - *Keep verifiable:* unchanged outcome verifier; apply advantages across segments; penalize high-entropy summaries (MMPO).
   - *Sources:* ReSum, SUPO, Context-Folding, IterResearch, CAMG, MMPO, AdaCoM.
5. **Instruction sharding across turns.** Splits a fully specified instruction into minimal units revealed one per turn; optionally withholds the core question.
   - *Easy → hard:* a GSM8K problem in one turn → 5 shards over 5 turns (−39% average across 15 LLMs; base models −19.4 to −64.9 pp).
   - *Keep verifiable:* enforce P1–P5; run CONCAT (≈95% of FULL expected) and drop failures; reuse the original verifier. Instruction Hierarchy's context synthesis is the same operator across privilege levels.
   - *Sources:* Lost in Conversation, RLAAR, 2606.12941, FiC, MAIGO, Instruction Hierarchy.
6. **Premature truncation and unsolvable variants.** Withholds a necessary shard, blocks all tool paths, or inserts a false premise; gold becomes abstain, stop or correct the premise.
   - *Easy → hard:* all 5 shards → cut after 3, gold "Abstain" (Qwen3-8B abstain score 33.5% → 73.4%). Or S3 all-paths-blocked tool episodes.
   - *Keep verifiable:* certify necessity (solver or ablation); cap the unsolvable ratio (RLAAR m=0.1; BENCH2ROBUST 25% S3); add an explicit positive reward because stock evaluators give none.
   - *Sources:* RLAAR, BENCH2ROBUST, LongMemEval, AskBench.
7. **Hidden spec behind an oracle user (category-targeted removal).** The agent gets a lossy spec; a simulator holds the full one.
   - *Easy → hard:* a full SWE-bench issue (43.8%) → one missing Error Info/Reproduction (23.7%; 36.8% with learned clarification). A ColBench description with the simulator holding reference code (Llama-3.1-8B: 6.9% single-turn → 22.4% collaborative).
   - *Keep verifiable:* hidden tests unchanged; restrict simulator answers to the full spec and audit for verbatim leakage; weight clarification rewards by measured information impact.
   - *Sources:* CLARITI, SWEET-RL/ColBench, CollabLLM, UserRL/UserBench, InfoPO.
8. **One-missing-variable (1-sufficient) underspecification.** Deletes exactly one necessary assignment from a solver-backed problem.
   - *Easy → hard:* a solvable PDDL instance → the same instance with one unobserved initial fact; the model must choose the one question that restores solvability (40–50% for current models).
   - *Keep verifiable:* the CSP or planner computes the sufficient question set exactly.
   - *Sources:* QuestBench, CIGAsk.
9. **Multi-interpretation ambiguity.** Keeps a query that has several valid readings, each a simulated user with its own gold.
   - *Easy → hard:* an unambiguous NQ question → an AmbigQA-style question where answering directly fails some users.
   - *Keep verifiable:* per-interpretation gold answers; label preferences by simulated next-turn outcomes; include unambiguous items where asking is wrong.
   - *Sources:* future-turn clarification preferences, CIGAsk, STaR-GATE.
10. **Split-information cooperation.** Partitions the seed's evidence between agents so no single view suffices.
    - *Easy → hard:* one agent sees the whole symbolic puzzle → two agents each see complementary halves (AsymPuzl). Or Mini → Simple Hanabi.
    - *Keep verifiable:* the union of views equals the original seed, so the original solver applies; check each view alone is insufficient. Note that strong models (GPT-5, Claude-4.0) solve AsymPuzl in two turns by dumping everything, so add bandwidth or turn limits.
    - *Sources:* AsymPuzl, MARSHAL, SWEET-RL.
11. **Discussion wrapper with planted partner errors.** Wraps a verifiable seed in multi-agent debate where some partners confidently argue a known-wrong answer.
    - *Easy → hard:* a single model solves a MATH item → it must disagree, convince and agree on the correct answer (Coral: up to +29.4% over single-agent CoT after training).
    - *Keep verifiable:* reward only gold-correct consensus; avoid learned-verifier-only rewards (MAPoRL) where gold exists.
    - *Sources:* MAPoRL, Coral, MAGRPO.
12. **Hidden-information game transformation.** Recasts content as a game whose hidden target or role is fixed by the environment.
    - *Easy → hard:* a vocabulary word → Adversarial Taboo. Or a document task → a spy game with a masked-input player (note 17).
    - *Keep verifiable:* rule-decided outcomes (string match, vote vs true role); role-aware or turn-level advantages.
    - *Sources:* SPAG, MARSHAL, Among Us RL, Werewolf RL, Social Gym, SpyRL (note 17).
13. **Indirect injection into untrusted slots, checked on state.** Inserts attacker text into tool outputs, emails, pages or files of an otherwise unchanged task.
    - *Easy → hard:* "summarize my emails" in a clean inbox (GPT-4o utility 69%) → the same task with an "important message" injection (57.7% targeted ASR).
    - *Keep verifiable:* deterministic utility and security functions on final state; attacker restricted to designated slots; randomized position (Meta-SecAlign) to prevent shortcut defenses.
    - *Sources:* AgentDojo, Meta-SecAlign, ARLAS, CoER, GPT-Red.
14. **Privilege-level conflict injection.** Pairs an easy high-priority instruction with a conflicting lower-priority message; includes aligned twins.
    - *Easy → hard:* "answer only in French" → the same with an online attacker's user or tool message demanding otherwise (GPT-5-Mini IH robustness 84.1% → 94.1% after training).
    - *Keep verifiable:* grader depends only on high-priority text; keep tasks IF-simple; keep an anti-over-refusal split.
    - *Sources:* Instruction Hierarchy, IH-Challenge, SecAlign.
15. **Tactic composition and multi-turn escalation, with benign twins.** Wraps a harmful seed in 2–7 mined tactics or a persona-driven multi-turn plan; generates vanilla and adversarial benign twins from the same seeds.
    - *Easy → hard:* a vanilla harmful request → an escalation plan reaching 96.2% ASR on Claude 3.7 Sonnet. A benign lookalike → an OR-Bench-Hard prompt refused by ≥3 frontier models.
    - *Keep verifiable:* intent-preservation filters or faithfulness rewards; ensemble-certified benign twins; toxic control set.
    - *Sources:* WildTeaming, X-Teaming, ActorBreaker, Self-RedTeam, OR-Bench, FalseReject.
16. **Tool-failure injection with scenario-controlled solvability.** Fault-injects tools and assigns each episode retry/switch/stop solvability.
    - *Easy → hard:* tau2-retail with perfect tools → 40% failing calls including silent ±10–25% value corruption, with the primary path blocked (69/70 pairs degrade, up to −46.7 pp).
    - *Keep verifiable:* deterministic blocking; injection budget (B≤5, ≤2 consecutive); verify against the uncorrupted state; explicit stop reward for S3.
    - *Sources:* BENCH2ROBUST (with note 06's COVERT/RODS).
17. **Adaptive adversary / population self-play as the difficulty escalator.** Replaces static attacks with an optimized or RL-trained attacker playing against current and past defenders.
    - *Easy → hard:* fixed "ignore previous" template (5.41% ASR on GPT-4o) → defense-aware GCG (>50% ASR against all 8 defenses) → RL-found fake-CoT injections (GPT-5.6 robustness 5.2% before adversarial training).
    - *Keep verifiable:* zero reward for invalid attacks; defender reward = completion AND resistance; historical-opponent populations for both roles; a held-out adaptive evaluator.
    - *Sources:* GPT-Red, ARLAS, CoER, Self-RedTeam, IH-Challenge, Adaptive Attacks Break Defenses, AutoInject, RETA, AdvEvo-MARL.

## Insights & pitfalls

- **Controls separate "hard" from "broken".** Delivery operators preserve labels only if the information is really all there. CONCAT (LiC), oracle-evidence reading (LongMemEval) and fully specified runs (CLARITI's 41.6–43.8%) should be computed per item. Items whose control fails are label errors.
- **Parametric shortcuts destroy memory tasks.** Filter closed-book-answerable items first (MemAgent removed ≈50%). Mem-α and UMA both score answers with a frozen reader over the built memory, which stops the writer from grading itself.
- **RL, not SFT, is what extrapolates horizon.** MEM1's SFT collapses beyond 6 objectives (0.088 at 6, 0.027 at 8, 0.000 at 16). MAPoRL similarly finds SFT on good collaboration samples does not induce collaboration. Use SFT only as a cold start (UserRL: >100% gains from cold start on some gyms).
- **Memory skills are competency-specific.** Single-retrieval agents transfer badly (MemAgent 0.198, MEM1 0.071 on MemoryAgentBench vs Mem-α 0.592). Mix retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding and update/forgetting tasks.
- **Curriculum gates can be too strict or too loose.** In RLAAR, ρ=1.0 stalls for 1000+ steps, ρ=0.8 reaches the hardest stage in 90, and ρ=0.6 collapses to 40.9. Tie the gate to a fraction of the model's own easy-view reward.
- **Degenerate constant policies are the default failure of ask/abstain/refuse operators.** CIGAsk reports prompted models asking every time or asking vaguely. IH-Challenge without its anti-over-refusal split loses 0.16 helpfulness. Self-RedTeam needs 50:50 benign seeds, and without hidden CoT Llama over-refuses (0.528 XSTest compliance). Balance solvable and unsolvable, and ambiguous and unambiguous, items.
- **Zero-variance groups are common in interaction tasks.** 38% of BENCH2ROBUST tasks had zero reward variance without partial credit, UserRL's Naive sparse turn rewards collapse training, and InfoPO targets exactly this. Add dense but anchored signals: partial credit, counterfactual information gain, belief-entropy penalties.
- **The simulator is part of the verifier.** Open-source simulators "get confused" (CollabLLM). Stronger simulators train faster (UserRL). Simulators may leak the hidden spec (CLARITI and SWEET-RL both flag it). Restrict simulators to the withheld spec, audit them, and evaluate under simulator shift (InfoPO). See note 17 for simulator-fidelity failures.
- **The easy view is a free teacher.** When sharded RL is too sparse, distill from the same model's CONCAT or history-cleaned view (FiC ≥92% recovery; MAIGO SHARDED/FULL 66.5% → 84.1%). Self-contamination from earlier wrong assistant turns is a trainable part of the gap (MAIGO).
- **Static adversarial benchmarks overstate robustness.** Adaptive GCG breaks all 8 IPI defenses at >50% ASR. Gemini's adaptive attacks match or beat non-adaptive ones in 16 of 24 cases. AutoInject breaks Meta-SecAlign-70B. RETA reports that near-zero static ASR collapses under adaptive attackers.
- **Single opponents collapse; populations do not.** GPT-Red attackers trained against one defender mode-collapse. Self-RedTeam's attacker-only variant puts 86.3% of attacks into one tactic. ARLAS without population training fails to reduce ASR. Keep historical-opponent pools for both roles (CoER).
- **For adversarial operators, label preservation means enforcing the threat model.** Restrict edits to designated slots and formats, reject invalid attacks (GPT-Red), keep base tasks IF-simple (IH-Challenge), and reward defenders for completion AND resistance, so "refuse everything" and "destroy the task" never pay.
- **Defenses learn positional shortcuts.** SecAlign's always-at-end injections taught "ignore the last instruction" and crushed agentic utility to 6.2%. Randomizing position alone restored +69.0 pp.
- **Capability does not buy robustness.** AgentDojo reports that more capable models tend to be easier to attack, and Gemini found better instruction-followers sometimes easier to attack. Injection difficulty therefore saturates more slowly than capability seeds, which makes it a durable source of GRPO signal.
- **Cooperative credit assignment differs from competitive.** Role-conditioned normalization that works for zero-sum games breaks cooperative Hanabi (MARSHAL). Use turn-level sum-then-normalize and agent-specific normalization.
- **Judge-scored social rewards need structure.** Holistic whole-conversation judging aligns with humans only 37.33% of the time (MultiChallenge baseline), vs 93.95% for instance-level rubrics. Sotopia-RL needs multi-dimensional, offline-attributed rewards. Prefer rule-decided games (Social Gym, SPAG, MARSHAL) when possible.
- **Split-information puzzles saturate for strong models.** Strong models share everything in two turns (AsymPuzl). Difficulty must come from bandwidth or turn limits, larger state, or unreliable partners, not asymmetry alone.

## Open problems & research opportunities

- **Composing operators on one seed.** No open pipeline delivers a verifiable seed as sharded turns, over a horizon beyond the window, with an injected tool output and an information-withholding partner, and measures how the knobs (N, K, H, injection budget, attacker strength) interact.
- **Automatic label-preservation certificates outside math and logic.** LiC needed 1–4 hours of manual work per task, 2606.12941 uses LLM verification, and CLARITI validated 50 of 1,500 rewrites. Solver-backed CONCAT equivalence, shard-necessity ablations and QuestBench-style sufficiency checks for code, SQL and tool tasks are missing.
- **Adaptive partners and users.** Cooperative work uses self-play copies or fixed simulators. Generating progressively harder partners (forgetful, noisy, contradictory, adversarially unhelpful) with verifiable outcomes, and measuring robustness to simulator shift, is barely explored. InfoPO and SEAD (note 17) are first steps.
- **Memory RL beyond QA.** Stacking agentic tasks (SWE, tool environments, research) where state from task i is needed at task i+k, with programmatic checks, is nascent (CAMG, proactive memory agent 2607.08716).
- **Principled calibration of interaction knobs.** Gates are hand-tuned (RLAAR ρ, BENCH2ROBUST phase thresholds, IH attacker budgets). Tying N/K/H/injection budget to a target pass rate, as difficulty-targeted generators do for content (note 09), has not been done for interaction structure.
- **Credit for correct abstention in evaluators.** Standard benchmark evaluators score S3-style impossible episodes as failures, discouraging the behaviour and making "always abstain" hard to detect.
- **Open GPT-Red-scale self-play.** Population attacker/defender training over released capability environments, reporting transfer to capability and to held-out adaptive attackers, does not exist in the open.
- **A unified contrastive generator from one seed pool.** It would produce benign sharded tasks, malicious multi-turn decompositions (X-Teaming, STAC/ToolHazard in note 06) and benign hard negatives (OR-Bench, FalseReject), so one model tracks intent across turns without over-refusing.
- **Fresh, programmatically labelled long-memory streams.** LongMemEval (500 questions) and LoCoMo (about 50 dialogues) are small and increasingly trained on. Ledger-style generators with program-chosen values could supply contamination-free training and evaluation at any H.
- **Verifiable social rewards at scale.** Rule-decided game suites (Social Gym, MINDGAMES) and hidden-role transformations (SpyRL, note 17) have not yet been used to train general assistants at scale, and their transfer to real collaboration is unmeasured.
